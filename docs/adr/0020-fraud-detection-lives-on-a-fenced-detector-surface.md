# Fraud detection lives on a fenced detector surface

The product now estimates the probability that a claim is fraudulent. This lives on a new **detector surface** — the Sweep Score, the Verdict, the Case File, and the panel that shows them — kept apart from the **investigation surface**: Genie's answers, pattern names, similarity reasons, timeline labels, Brief sentences, and the interface copy around them. This supersedes the charter's "Not a fraud detection engine. Not a fraud score." The charter still refuses underwriting, pricing, adjudication, a general dashboard, a general SQL chatbot, and policy administration; it now adds that the product investigates policy history and, on the detector surface, estimates a probability — always about one claim, always beside that probability, never about a person.

The change is scoped to a second surface rather than lifted across the whole product because the investigation surface's guarantees are load-bearing elsewhere and none of them needed to move. ADR-0009 makes a Noteworthy Pattern a named rule, never a score. ADR-0014 requires associational language and bans causal claims because the dataset is synthetic and its patterns are deliberately seeded. ADR-0018 holds the Brief to restating facts and never weighing them. All three still hold, unchanged, on the investigation surface after this decision; a detector surface that names fraud and carries a probability is a different thing living beside them, not a replacement for any of them.

Two alternatives were rejected. Lifting the ban everywhere — letting Genie, the pattern names, or the Brief say "fraud" — would let those surfaces characterise a person, which is exactly what ADR-0009 and ADR-0018 exist to prevent. A rule that fires states a fact; a probability weighs one. Conflating the two on the investigation surface would make every existing Genie answer and every Brief sentence suspect. Scoring internally while keeping soft words in the user-facing copy — computing a real fraud probability but only ever showing "noteworthy" — was rejected the other way: it says less than the product knows, on a surface built to be looked at directly, which is a worse dishonesty than naming the estimate plainly on a surface fenced for exactly that purpose.

## Consequences

- `BANNED_VOCABULARY` splits into `ACCUSATORY_TERMS` (fraud, fraudulent, suspicious, scheme, deceptive, risk score, anomaly, anomalous, red flag — banned on the investigation surface, permitted on the detector surface) and `ALWAYS_BANNED` (guilty, predicts, causes, leads to, increases the risk of — banned on both). `vocabulary_violations(text, surface="investigation")` defaults to today's behaviour, so E18, the Brief sentence check and Genie contract QC-13 are unaffected by this decision.
- On the detector surface, the rule against characterising a person is a fixed set of regular expressions, not language understanding: a person noun (`policyholder`, `customer`, `insured`, `claimant`, `driver`) as the grammatical subject of an accusatory term, or a customer's name appearing at all. It catches the obvious sentences; the convention carries the rest.
- `ALWAYS_BANNED` applies on every surface without exception. No probability the detector produces is ever framed as a settled fact ("guilty"), a prediction, or a cause.
- The Verdict is a separate artefact from the Brief, not a fifth Brief section. ADR-0018's rule — the Brief restates facts in the approved vocabulary and never weighs them — is undisturbed; the weighing now has its own place to live instead of leaking into the Brief.
- The human Disposition remains the only decision on record. A Verdict is evidence the reviewer sees alongside the Brief, not a decision that stands in for one.
- The generator's own source-vocabulary test, which forbids accusatory terms across its top-level modules, exempts exactly `generator/truth.py` and `generator/validate_truth.py` by name, citing this decision. Every other generator module — including `generator/notes.py`, which authors the claim notes — stays under the test, because notes are investigation-surface text today.
